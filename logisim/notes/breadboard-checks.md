# To check on the breadboard

What the paper analysis can't prove; measure it on the breadboard with a scope. Add each item with the section that needs it and why.

| # | Section | What to measure | Pass | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | address section (2026-11-05, addr-report.md item 4) | At a `clk` rising edge, how long the control register's outputs (`addr_lat_ld_n`, `addr_pc_ld_n`, `addr_sp_ld_n`) and the 377s' D inputs stay unchanged after the edge, at the 377 pins | /E ≥ 5 ns, D ≥ 3 ns (74HCT377 hold, TI SCHS184C) | TI gives no minimum CP → Q for the control register's 574 (typ 15 ns), so the 377's hold times can't be proven on paper (addr-decisions.md A4). |
| B2 | memory (2026-11-05, Gianni's answer 5, ir-mem-decisions.md M1) | At the RAM pins (AS6C1008 WE#, A0–A15): the WE# low pulse width, and the time from the last address change (after the `clk` edge) to WE# falling | pulse ≥ 45 ns (tWP; 174 ns on paper at 25 °C), address stable ≥ 0 ns before WE# falls (tAS; 25 ns on paper at 25 °C, −2 at 85 °C with FF1 at max and FF2 at 0) and until WE# rises (tWR 0) | The pulse and the setup depend on the spread between the two flip-flops of the clock generator's 74HCT74 and on the minimum delays, which TI doesn't specify. |
| B3 | control unit (2026-11-05, Gianni's acceptance of U5, control-requirements.md C24) | (a) At power-up and with the reset button held: every pipeline 574 output at its pulled level (idle word, `cu_cond_sel` = 11, `cu_force` = 0), no bus with two drivers (supply current, `bus_data` / `bus_addr` / bus A / bus B on the scope: no mid-level). (b) After release: `rst_rc` falls before `rst_early_n` rises, and the reset word is on the 574 outputs at the last `clk` edge with `rst_n` low (PC = 0000, SP = FE00 after the reset). (c) The pull resistors' rise and fall at the 574 /OE edges, against the next `clk` edge | (a) no contention, current within the board's normal figure; (b) PC = 0000, SP = FE00 after 100 resets (power-up and button); (c) settled before the next edge | The 574s have no clear, and `rst_rc` is asynchronous to `clk`: the paper analysis gives the order of the edges but not the pulled lines' RC time on the real board. |
| B4 | memory (2026-11-10, ir-mem-decisions.md M8) | During the boot copy, at the two A15 changes (the step that copies 8000 after 7FFF, and the step that copies 0000 again after FFFF), trigger on A15 and probe: both ROMs' /CE (pin 20), one `mem_rom_d` bit whose two bytes differ (program a test image with 7FFF = 00, 8000 = FF, FFFF = FF, 0000 = 00), and the ROMs' supply (VCC at their pins, or the current through a 1 Ω shunt in their VCC) | The bit has its new level within 150 ns of A15 (tCE); the in-between level (both ROMs driving) lasts ≤ 50 ns (tDF); VCC at the ROMs stays within 4.5–5.5 V; RAM[7FFF], [8000], [FFFF], [0000] read back as programmed | With /OE = `mem_rom_oe_n` (M8) the two ROMs overlap only in the boot, for up to tDF = 50 ns at each A15 change: harmless to the data on paper (sampled at 322 ns), but a short between two outputs. Fallback: a 100 Ω series resistor network per ROM on `mem_rom_d` |
| B5 | I/O (2026-11-11, Q-C1 and the A8–A15 pull-downs, iod-requirements.md I3 / I6) | (a) At the end of an IN step and of a vector step: the overlap between the mailbox's driver turning off (RX / STATUS 595 or 244, the EVT 595s) and the next step's `bus_data` driver turning on (the RAM read buffer of F0 / IE6), at a `bus_data` pin; supply current spike. (b) In the vector step: A8–A15 at the RAM pins (they hold the latch's last value, then decay through 10 kΩ) and the RAM's / ROMs' supply current | (a) overlap ≤ about 60 ns, no glitch on the next read; (b) no oscillation, the step after reads normally | Paper can't prove either: no minimum delays (Q-C1); a slow edge through the pull-downs if the latch left a 1 there (it loads 0000 at the same edge it turns off, a race) |
| B6 | I/O processor, native commands (2026-11-11, Gianni's answer 7, Documentation/Peripherals/Native/Reference.md) | On the TTGO VGA32 with FabGL: (a) the hook that counts frames (the VGA controller's vertical blank), with a GPIO toggled there on the scope; (b) the 256 × 192 modeline: the monitor locks to it, and its frame rate; (c) `drawLine` against the emulator's line: draw the test lines of `iop` tests (all 8 octants, both ends, steep and flat) and compare `GET_PIXEL` read-back with the emulator's pixels; (d) `GET_PIXEL` after a drawing (`waitCompletion` first) returns the palette index drawn | (a) one toggle per frame, nothing missed while drawing; (b) the monitor shows the full picture, 60 Hz ± 1 % (else VSYNC's `CLOCKS_PER_SEC` changes); (c) the same pixels; (d) every index 0–15 reads back | FabGL's docs don't settle these: the custom mode and the vertical-blank hook are firmware choices, and the emulator's Bresenham line may round differently from FabGL's |
| B7 | I/O, the TTGO VGA32's cables (2026-11-14, iod-decisions.md "The TTGO VGA32 wired to the card") | (a) MBX_SEL at U7 pin 3 and RCLK_EVT at U6 / U9 pin 12: the rising edge through the TTGO's 4.7 kΩ pull-up, with the PS/2 cable fitted; (b) SCK at U8 pin 2 and U3 pin 11: overshoot and ringing over the ribbon, with the ESP32's drive strength set low; (c) +3V3 at J5.8 with the HBC-2 and the VGA running; (d) flashing the TTGO over USB while the HBC-2 runs | (a) 10–90 % rise under 500 ns (74HCT input limit at 4.5 V), one clean transition, no second edge; (b) no ringing back across 0.8–2.0 V, so the 595s and the 165 see one edge per bit; (c) 3.3 V ± 5 %; (d) the flash and the boot succeed, the HBC-2's +5V doesn't move when USB is plugged in, and the CPU sees no spurious RX or EVT (`rx_full` / `evt_pend` stay 0) | Paper can't give the cable's capacitance or ringing. The 74HCT74 / 595 clock inputs have no Schmitt trigger, and a slow or ringing edge can clock twice. The TTGO's supply comes through D3 and its own diode OR. |
